Safety first

Michigan safety center

Protect people, property, and the jobsite with practical guidance aligned to Michigan field electrical work expectations.

Michigan non-union rules at a glance

  • Union status does not change MIOSHA or OSHA obligations on a jobsite.
  • Electrical installation work should be performed by properly licensed individuals or supervised apprentices per Michigan requirements.
  • Keep worker qualification, task training, and toolbox talk records current.
  • Use written procedures for energized work justification, when unavoidable.

Core PPE and electrical controls

  • Hard hat, eye protection, high-visibility wear, gloves, and protective footwear.
  • Use voltage-rated tools and test instruments suited to the circuit category.
  • De-energize whenever possible and apply lockout/tagout before servicing.
  • Verify absence of voltage before touch using an approved test process.

Michigan underground utility safety

  • Submit an 811 ticket before excavation and wait the required notice window.
  • Walk and document utility marks before breaking ground.
  • Use caution digging within tolerance zones and expose utilities safely.
  • Stop immediately and notify stakeholders if markings are missing or conflicting.

Daily crew briefing checklist

  • Review scope, hazards, and stop-work triggers before startup.
  • Confirm traffic control, barricades, and exclusion zones.
  • Inspect cords, GFCI protection, connectors, and tool guards.
  • Assign one person to capture near misses and corrective actions.

Incident and near-miss response

  • Call 911 for emergencies and protect the scene from additional harm.
  • Shut down energy sources and isolate unsafe equipment.
  • Report incidents and near misses the same shift with factual details.
  • Complete a corrective-action review before resuming similar work.

Supervisor documentation to keep onsite

  • Daily job hazard analysis and pre-task briefing sign-in.
  • Qualified worker list and current training records.
  • Utility locate ticket details and mark verification notes.
  • Equipment inspection logs and lockout/tagout verification sheets.

Important note

Use current rules for final decisions

This page is a field aid, not legal advice. Always verify current MIOSHA, Michigan licensing, and company policy requirements before starting work.
